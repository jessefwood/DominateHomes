# Abbie Grossman — Sourcing map and purchase budget

Port St. Lucie, FL 34987 (Valencia Parc at Riverland). Plan 643 "Bianca," 2,799 a/c sq ft.
**Goods only — no design fee, no labor beyond delivery and assembly.**

All dollar figures are planning bands built from current retail price points, not quotes. Nothing here goes in a client proposal until it's been priced against live SKUs.

## 1. Where the goods come from

### Ships anywhere — the backbone of the order

| Source | What it's for here | Notes |

### Delivers locally, on their own trucks — the ones that solve the storage problem

| Source | Distance | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Rooms To Go**, Jensen Beach | ~30 min | Florida's volume player. Large in-stock inventory delivered in days, not months. This is the fallback if the completion date slides forward and a room has to be filled fast. |
| **City Home (City Furniture)**, Stuart | ~35 min | A real step up on construction quality. Florida chain, own delivery fleet, will usually hold a paid order until the house is ready. |
| **Family Furniture of America**, Stuart | ~35 min | Independent, 4.8 across 2,200+ reviews. Worth a trade-account conversation for a whole-house order — independents will discount where the chains won't. |
| **Baer's Furniture** (Stuart / Palm Beach) | ~40 min | The one to check for patio and for anything coastal-transitional. |

### Walk-in, buy-it-when-you-see-it

| Source | Distance | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Homesense**, Tradition | 5 min | The large-format TJX store — actual furniture, big rugs, oversized art, patio. The single most useful store on this list for the "light" rooms. |
| **HomeGoods**, St. Lucie West | 12 min | Lamps, mirrors, baskets, accessories, bath. No inventory system — go often, buy on sight. |

**The one thing to get right:** everything on this list except HomeGoods/Homesense charges **7% sales tax** (6% state + 1% St. Lucie surtax), and the county 1% only applies to the first $5,000 of any single item. Budget 7% and you'll be slightly over, which is the right direction.

### How to actually buy it

HomeGoods has no online inventory, no reorders, and no way to hold a piece. If you see it and it works, it goes in the cart — the alternative is that it's gone. That cuts both ways:

- Go with **a written list and a tape measure**, organised by room, with the wall dimensions written down.
- Buy the **large art first**. Small pieces are always there; a good 48" canvas is not.
- **One trip per room**, not one trip per item. Buying a room's worth at once is the only way the objects relate to each other.
- Keep receipts. 30 days to return, and the miss rate on this kind of shopping is a real 15–20%.
- Don't start until the **rug and the sofa fabric are locked**. Accessories are the last layer, not the first.
- Two visits a month for three months will produce a better house than one heroic trip, but only if the anchor pieces are already placed.

## 4. The all-in number

| | Budget-smart | Mid (recommended) | Step-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goods, all rooms incl. art and decor | $26,800 | $36,500 | $88,400 |
| Freight, delivery, assembly, install (8–12%) | $2,150 | $3,600 | $10,600 |
| Sales tax @ 7% | $2,030 | $2,800 | $6,900 |
| Contingency (8–10%) | $2,500 | $4,300 | $10,600 |
| **Total purchase** | **≈ $33,500** | **≈ $47,000** | **≈ $116,500** |

**The one to plan against is roughly $47,000.** That's the number that matches "not over the top, furnish it nicely without breaking the bank" while still buying a house that photographs well — real fibre rugs, performance fabric on the sofa, proper mattresses, and window treatments in every room.

$32,000 is achievable but it spends its margin on the invisible things. At that level the compromises land on mattress quality, cushion fabric on the patio, and rug size — and a too-small rug is the single fastest way to make an expensive room look cheap.

$114,000 is what the same house costs specified at designer retail with custom window treatments. It's in the table so the range is honest, not because it's the recommendation.

### Where the money actually is
Five lines carry 58% of the mid-tier budget: **window treatments ($5,000), primary suite ($5,000), dining room ($3,700), great room ($3,600), patio ($3,300)**, with art and decor behind them at $5,200. Every other room is noise by comparison. If the budget has to come down, it comes down in those or it doesn't come down.

### Three ways to use it, and what each costs

Baseline is the mid tier, $36,500 in goods.

**Scenario A — Everything stays.** Their sofa and loveseat anchor the great room, slipcovered. Club room becomes a reading retreat with the carved table and Tiffany lamp. Baroque mirror stays over the foyer console.
- Removes the new great-room sofa and two chairs: **−$2,000**
- Adds a second club-room chair and ottoman to fill the space: **+$400**
- **Net ≈ $34,900 goods · all-in ≈ $45,000**
- The catch isn't money. Keeping the traditional pieces pushes the house toward direction 02 or 03; directions 01 and 04 reject them. Choose the direction around the pieces or release the pieces — don't fight it.

**Scenario B — Option 1 as written.** New great-room seating; their sofa and loveseat move to the club room slipcovered; traditional pieces placed but subordinate.
- **$36,500 goods · all-in ≈ $47,000**
- The recommendation. Best room gets the best furniture; the club room gets a real identity instead of sitting half empty.

**Scenario C — Lean reuse.** Keep the strong pieces only — media console, console table, white lacquer bedroom set, all nine art pieces. Release the sofa, loveseat, baroque mirror, carved table and Tiffany lamp.
- Drops the slipcovers: **−$320**
- Club room now needs its own seating: **+$1,100**
- Foyer needs a statement mirror: **+$250**
- **Net ≈ $37,500 goods · all-in ≈ $48,500**
- Costs the most and owns the least. Only worth it if she's already lukewarm on those pieces.

### The honest read

All three scenarios land inside a **$3,500 spread on a $47,000 project** — about 7%. The reuse decision is not a budget lever. It's a taste and attachment decision, and it should be made on that basis. What actually moves the number is window treatments, mattress tier, and whether the patio gets solution-dyed cushions.

Say that at the consult. It frees her to keep the sofa because she likes it rather than because she thinks she's saving money, and it stops the whole meeting from getting stuck on the seating.

### Where to spend and where to save
**Spend:** rug size in the great room, sofa fabric and frame, mattresses, patio cushion fabric, dining and primary light fixtures, one oversized piece of art per major room.
**Save:** case goods in the guest rooms, side tables, counter stools, bath, everything accessory-scale — and reframe rather than replace.

## 6. Before any of this gets ordered

1. **Storage and delivery.** She's in a rental and the house isn't finished. A sofa with a ten-week lead and nowhere to land is the most common way a project like this goes sideways. Either the order is staged against a confirmed completion date, or a local receiving warehouse goes in the budget at $300–800/month. City Furniture and Family Furniture will typically hold a paid order; Wayfair will not.
2. **Completion date.** Everything upstream depends on it. Custom upholstery is 8–16 weeks; schedule backwards from move-in, not forwards from today.
3. **Existing guest bed size.** Decides whether the 2nd bedroom mattress is a queen or a king, and whether the piece even fits the 12 × 12.
4. **Window treatment inclusions in the GL contract.** Single biggest swing line in the estimate.
5. **Art measurements.** Every size on file is estimated from a photograph. Scale decides which wall a piece can hold.
6. **Whether the island takes 3 stools or 4.**
