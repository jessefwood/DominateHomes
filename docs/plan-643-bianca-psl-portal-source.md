## 2. Direction — decided 9/14/2026

**A blend of Modern Coastal and Traditional (New England) Coastal.** Boho and organic were ruled out on sight.

**Hard no:** wicker · woven and weave texture · bamboo · striped chairs · end-of-bed bench · round side tables

**Loved:** the swivel chairs from the organic board — the same ones used in their Silverthorne house. Now roughly double what they cost two years ago.

**Metals:** black and brushed nickel, because the plumbing fixtures are silver.

**Palette:** approved without edits. Sand / oyster / driftwood field, sea glass as the only real colour, black in thin deliberate lines.

**Abbie's own summary:** *"Make it look Florida, not Colorado."*

**The tension to manage:** they lean traditional, and traditional is the most expensive style to execute. Abbie knows it — *"I don't want to go expensive, but then that's what I love"* — and resolved it herself: *"I don't have to go to the most expensive maker for the couch and the chairs."* Name brand is not the point. The look is.

### Hanging rules

- Centre line **57–60"** from the floor on an open wall. Never changes with ceiling height.
- **10-ft ceilings lie to you.** Above ~63" to centre, art floats. Fill height with a tall plant or lamp instead.
- Over furniture: **6–10" of air** between frame bottom and sofa back / console top / headboard.
- Art or group spans **two-thirds** the width of the furniture below it.
- Groups are **one rectangle.** Lay out on the floor, centre the whole outline. Gaps: 3–4" small frames, 5–6" large.
- The three Peter Lik pieces need studs or a cleat. Everything else is light.

## 7. Cost context — what changed since the Colorado job

The Colorado job ran roughly $30,000 for two bedrooms and a family room. This house is 13 rooms. Most of the gap is scope, not inflation.

**Bridge from $47,000 back down:**

| Line | Amount | Likely in the old number? |
|---|---|---|
| Core furniture, main rooms | $19,400 | Yes |
| Mattresses × 3 | $3,600 | Maybe |
| Patio, both zones | $3,300 | Often phase two |
| Window treatments | $5,000 | Usually separate scope |
| Art, mirrors, decor | $5,200 | Client usually shops herself |
| Freight + assembly | $3,600 | Sometimes embedded |
| Sales tax | $2,800 | Usually embedded |
| Contingency | $4,300 | Almost never quoted |

Core furniture plus mattresses = $23,000 — inside the old range. Add ~12% for two years of price movement and the identical scope today is **$26,000–29,000**.

**Inflation, actual:** a 25% Section 232 tariff on imported upholstered wooden furniture took effect 10/14/2025 and retailers passed it straight through. Living/kitchen/dining furniture CPI ran ~1% YoY in early 2025, peaked at 9.5% that August, then cooled to ~1.5% by May 2026 and 1.1% in July. The spike has already worked through — not a rising market now, a market after a step change.

**Patio runs hotter than average.** As of 4/6/2026 the steel and aluminum tariffs apply to full customs value rather than metal content — hits aluminum-frame outdoor furniture harder than wood case goods.

## 8. Ordering timing — the December decision

The escalation from 25% to 30% on upholstered furniture is currently scheduled for **1/1/2027** (already postponed once).

**Do not put a blanket tariff percentage on the quote.** Reasons:
- 5 percentage points is on *customs value*, roughly a third of retail. Passed through it's ~1.5–2.5% on the sticker.
- The tariff covers upholstered wooden furniture, cabinets, vanities. Dining tables, bed frames, coffee tables and case goods are generally outside it — maybe $4,000 of a $36,500 order.
- There is already a 10% contingency. Adding a tariff line double-counts the same risk.

**Do this instead — sequence, then protect with a clause.**

Vendors lock price at order acknowledgement, not delivery. Put upholstery in **before 12/31**; case goods, rugs, lighting and decor can wait for January.

> Prices quoted are valid for 30 days and are confirmed at vendor order acknowledgement. Orders placed after that window are subject to vendor pricing in effect at the time of order. Any government-imposed tariff, duty or surcharge levied after acknowledgement is passed through at documented cost.

**The stronger argument for December isn't tariffs.** Vendors reset price lists January 1 as routine practice, tariff or not. And Chinese New Year falls early February 2027, shutting factories two to three weeks. Order in December, price is locked and production beats the shutdown. Order in January, you're behind everyone who didn't.

## 11. Portal build spec

What the client-facing portal needs to do. Abbie signs in and works through it.

### Two client rules that shape the whole interface

1. **Never more than 3 options per item.** Stated twice. Her previous decorator had the same rule — *"it was hard enough with 3."* The selection UI should make it structurally impossible to show a fourth.
2. **The quote must break out Davina's expenses separately** from the furnishing spend. Asked for directly. Two distinct totals, always visible, never blended.

### Core screens

| Screen | Function |
|---|---|
| **Dashboard** | Where the project stands. Phase, what's waiting on her, what's waiting on Davina, days to install. |
| **Room by room** | Each room with dimensions, tier, contents list, budget band, and selection status. Click into a room to make decisions. |
| **Selections** | The 3-options-max picker, per item. Photo, price, lead time, dimensions. She picks one, it locks, status updates. |
| **Approvals** | Written sign-off per room before anything orders. Timestamped. Custom is non-returnable — the UI should say so at the point of approval. |
| **Budget** | Running total against the allocation. Furnishing spend and Davina's expenses as two separate columns. Spent / committed / remaining. |
| **Her pieces** | The reuse inventory with keep/release status and destination room. Lets her change her mind in one place instead of over text. |
| **Art** | The pieces, sizes, reframe status, and which wall each lands on. |
| **Open items** | Her list and Davina's list, side by side. The thing she owes with a field to answer it inline. |
| **Timeline** | The dated milestones from section 9, with the December ordering deadline called out. |
| **Order tracker** | Once procurement starts — what's ordered, acknowledged, in transit, received, damaged. |

### Data model notes

- Every selection needs: item ID, room, category, 3 option records, chosen option, status (`pending` / `chosen` / `approved` / `ordered` / `received`), price, lead time, vendor, non-returnable flag.
- Every room needs: dimensions, tier, rug size, budget band (low/mid/high), item list, approval status + timestamp.
- Every reuse piece needs: photo, dimensions, condition, keep/release status, destination room, replacement value.
- Budget lines need a `type` field — `furnishing` or `expense` — so the two totals never blend.
- Approvals need a snapshot of prices at the moment of signing, not a live reference. Prices move.

### Tone for client-facing copy

Natural, personable, speaking directly to her. No em dashes. Few inline links, especially near the top. Written the way Davina actually talks, not the way a system writes.
